# Applying the disciplines to teaching and consulting

**Status: notes, outside the framework.** Nothing here constrains the repo, and none of it has been
tried. Written in response to the question of whether these methods transfer to an educational and
consulting business.

## What transfers

**Falsifier before the engagement, not after.** State at the start what would show the engagement
failed. The chronic disease of consulting is unfalsifiable success claims — "improved alignment",
"raised maturity" — and the fix is the discipline in `docs/status.md`: record the falsifier before
the evidence exists, then check against it. It also protects the consultant: a criterion agreed in
week one cannot be relocated in week twelve.

**Criticality → required evidence, decided once.** `azimuth/standards/verification.md` is a policy
pattern, not a testing idea: state per tier what a claim owes, once, rather than negotiating per
claim. In a curriculum: which assertions need a citation, which need a worked demonstration, which
need neither.
It avoids both failure modes — everything cited is unreadable, nothing cited is punditry — and
D6.1's argument for why a default is legitimate here transfers intact.

**`Residual:` + `Accepted:` in every proposal.** The sharpest commercial transfer. Nearly every
consulting dispute is a residual nobody wrote down. A required section stating *what this engagement
knowingly does not cover, and why that is acceptable for you now* is both honest and disarming:
people who are bluffing do not enumerate their gaps.

**Decided / proposed / open, marked.** Clients routinely read a consultant's opinion as a settled
finding. Distinguishing them in the document is rare enough to be a differentiator.

**Marked revisions.** `*(revised)*`, `*(supersedes …)*`, keeping enough of the earlier reasoning to
show why it changed. For a knowledge base that outlives an engagement this is worth more than the
recommendations themselves, and it stops the same question being re-litigated quarterly.

**For the educational half: `toothless` imports directly into assessment design.** The judging
question — *would this be passed by someone who had not learned the thing?* — is the most useful
question available about an exercise, and most technical training fails it. An exercise a student
can pattern-match is a test tagged `universal` over a scripted case.

## Two cautions

**A falsifier in a commercial document is close to a warranty.** "This engagement failed if X" can
be held against you in ways you did not price, particularly when X depends on the client's data,
access or decisions. State falsifiers as shared criteria with client-side preconditions named, not
as unilateral guarantees. This is the one place where importing the discipline naively costs money.

**The cost gradient follows you.** D18.1 generalizes: a standard that is expensive to satisfy
honestly is cheap to satisfy dishonestly. Require a residual in every proposal and within two
quarters some will read "Residual: none identified". The countermeasure is the same one the
framework uses — a review pass by someone who did not write the document, and a periodic check of
whether the section produces content or ceremony.

## What would be self-defeating

**Do not teach or sell this framework as validated methodology.** `docs/framework.md` says outright
that most of its falsifiers are *never tested* and *never measured*, that the surviving novelty
claim is demonstrated once, by its author, on one spec, and that annotation cost has never been
weighed against defects found. The session that produced these notes added evidence in both
directions: the quantification field caught four dishonest tags and also produced them.

Selling that as established produces exactly the outcome `AGENTS.md` warns about for overreaching
terminology — the first rigorous reader who pushes on it costs more credibility than the claim ever
bought. Selling it as a working hypothesis with a visible revision history is both honest and the
better teaching material, because a decision that was made, falsified and revised can be shown end
to end. That is rare, and this repo has several.
